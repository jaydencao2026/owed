# Putting Owed on its own free website

The microphone check only works when Owed runs on its own website. The claude.ai link blocks microphones. Both options below are free and take about 10 minutes. Neither needs any coding.

**Upload these files:** `index.html`, `core.js`, `audio-data.js`, `pinyin-data.js`. You can also add `README.md`, `RECORDING-CREDITS.md` and `HOSTING.md`. Don't upload `test.js`; the site doesn't need it.

---

## Option 1: GitHub Pages (good for the trial)

1. Go to [github.com](https://github.com) and create a free account.
2. Click **+** (top right) → **New repository**.
   - Name: `owed`
   - Choose **Public**, then click **Create repository**.
3. On the next page, click **uploading an existing file**. Drag in the files listed above, then click **Commit changes**.
4. Open **Settings** → **Pages** (left menu).
   - Under "Build and deployment," set **Source** to **Deploy from a branch**.
   - Set **Branch** to **main** and the folder to **/ (root)**, then click **Save**.
5. Wait 1–2 minutes and refresh. Your link appears at the top, like `https://YOUR-NAME.github.io/owed/`.

**Note:** GitHub Pages is free for projects and prototypes. GitHub's rules say it isn't meant for running a business, so if Owed starts charging money, move to Option 2.

---

## Option 2: Cloudflare Pages (good for later, including commercial use)

1. Go to [pages.cloudflare.com](https://pages.cloudflare.com) and create a free account.
2. Choose **Create a project** → **Direct Upload**.
3. Name it `owed`, drag in the same files, and click **Deploy**.
4. Your link looks like `https://owed.pages.dev`.

---

## After it's online

- Open the link on a phone in **Chrome** (Android) or **Safari** (iPhone).
- Go to **Focus time** → **Practice words** → **Turn on microphone check**, and allow the microphone when the phone asks.
- Tap **🎤 Say it** and say a word to test it.

**To update the site later,** upload the changed files again the same way. In GitHub, open the repository and choose **Add file** → **Upload files**.

**Privacy reminder:** families' records and recordings stay on their own phones. The website only holds the app itself. Before a public launch, have a lawyer check the microphone feature against COPPA, the US law protecting kids under 13. The browser sends the child's voice to Google or Apple to turn it into text.
